Content Summary

Coombs & Gell classification (1963): four types of hypersensitivity reactions, based on the type of immunity (humoral × cellular), the predominant effector and the time of onset.

Type Mechanism Antibody/Cell Time Clinical examples
I Immediate, IgE-mediated IgE, mast cells, basophils Minutes Anaphylaxis, allergic asthma, allergic rhinitis
II Cytotoxic / cytolytic IgG, IgM, complement Minutes to hours Autoimmune hemolytic anemia, hemolytic disease of the newborn
III Immune complexes IgG, IgM, complement 3–8 hours Serum sickness, glomerulonephritis
IV Delayed, cell-mediated T cells (Th1, Th17, CTL) 24–72 hours Contact dermatitis, tuberculin test

Integrating concept — Type 2 Inflammation: IgE-mediated allergic diseases (asthma, rhinitis, atopic dermatitis) share a common substrate: the production of the cytokines IL-4, IL-5 and IL-13 by Th2 lymphocytes and ILC2. This concept underpins the development of biologics (omalizumab, mepolizumab, dupilumab).

Best Teaching Practices Applied

  • Revised Bloom's Taxonomy (Anderson & Krathwohl, 2001): objectives structured by cognitive level — Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create.
  • Active methodologies (DCN 2025, Art. 18): case study with problematization, in small groups, with progressive ("sliced") reasoning.
  • Programmatic assessment (DCN 2025, Art. 36): continuous feedback, covering the three domains of competence — cognitive, psychomotor and attitudinal.
  • Teaching–service–community integration (DCN 2025, Art. 22): clinical case set in primary care/ESF, with public health actions.
